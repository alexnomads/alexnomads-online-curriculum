01Business Problem

Which audience's want do you ignore?

The bot matched esports players for prizes. Small scale, working. The question: could it become a real consumer app?

Three audiences existed, and they didn't want the same thing:

BlueStacks' desktop base · Microtransaction-obsessed gamers · Influencers & content creators

Build for the loudest one, burn the $25M raise. Miss the real signal, no app at all.

02Methodology & Rationale

What they asked for vs. what made them stay.

Three panels. One goal: what makes them stay, not what they say they want.

Didn't Build For

  • The loudest feature requests
  • Deeper IAP mechanics alone
  • A generic mobile gaming app

Built Instead

  • Esports tournament format with real prizes
  • Creator-driven content distribution loop
  • A bridge from Discord habit to a real app

03Key Trade-offs

Growth first. Extraction second.

What the loudest signal got wrong

Monetization-first roadmap, before an audience existed to monetize.

What changed

Creator and tournament signal got prioritized. Monetization depth got sequenced for after retention was proven.

Acted on. The bot became an app. The niche became a growth engine.

01
Host Tournaments
What it does

Create or join real-money and free-to-play tournaments across 300+ titles, matched in real time. A tournament went live every 60 seconds at peak.

How we got here

The original ask, validated across all three panels from day one. Nobody disagreed this was the core loop.

🔴
02
Create Live Stream
What it does

Go live with built-in editing, emoji reactions, and mini-games so fans stay engaged during a match, not just after it.

How we got here

Creators told us clip-sharing wasn't enough. Their audience was already showing up live for tournaments, and we built the stage where they already stood.

🎬
03
Upload Short Video
What it does

Post highlight clips, memes, and match moments to a dedicated feed inside the app.

How we got here

Surfaced from behavioral data: players were screen-recording wins and sharing them off-platform. We brought that habit in-house instead of losing it.

💬
04
Chat With Fans
What it does

Dedicated chat rooms and live calls: jokes, memes, strategy talk, all without leaving the app.

How we got here

One-on-one conversations kept surfacing the same want: a place to talk to fans that wasn't buried in a separate Discord server.

🎟️
05
Monetize With Fan Pass
What it does

Daily, weekly, or monthly fan subscriptions unlocking exclusive tournaments, badges, and perks, sold directly to a creator's followers.

How we got here

The clearest signal of all: players were already asking how to make money from the audience they'd built. This answered it directly.

04In Hindsight

Stated preference isn't signal. Behavior is.

Most intelligence candidates run the panels, write the findings, hand over the deck.

I stayed in the loop until the intelligence changed what actually shipped, then stayed long enough to catch a second signal nobody asked about. The creator-monetization evolution wasn't in any panel's feedback. It came from watching what users did after we gave them tournaments.

I don't hand off intelligence and walk away. I stay in the loop until it actually changes what ships.

The honest gap

When I overrode Panel 2's signal, I couldn't quantify how wrong it was. I couldn't say "this panel predicts 15% of retention variance vs 60% from Panel 3." I just knew from pattern-matching. That works when you're close to the product. It doesn't scale to 13 markets with a Director of Research asking you to show your work. That's the gap, and it's the specific reason a structured intelligence function exists.

The loudest audience isn't always the right one.

Knowing which one to build for is the job.
